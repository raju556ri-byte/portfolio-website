Propellant Combustion Research at IIT Bombay

Experimental combustion studies of solid, slurry, gel and hybrid propellants at the Propellant Research Laboratory, Department of Aerospace Engineering, IIT Bombay, under Prof. Nagendra Kumar.

My role

I worked with the laboratory’s research scholars on several studies. My work covered preparing propellant samples, running and recording laser-ignition and Crawford burner tests, formulating and burning gel propellants, and processing the data. I also supported and observed the assembly and testing of a hybrid motor.

Laser ignition of AP/HTPB with nano-aluminum

Laser ignition lights a propellant without contact, but many propellants absorb laser light poorly. I tested ammonium perchlorate (AP) and HTPB composite propellant containing nano-aluminum (nAl) in a chamber pressurized with nitrogen. A piezoelectric sensor, a photodiode and a pressure transducer detected ignition, while laser power, laser-on time and the ignition sequence were varied.

Ignition was inconsistent at 10–20% laser power and became consistent from 40%. At 60–100% it was faster and more repeatable.

Crawford burner tests: slurry and electrically controlled propellants

I prepared an HTPB-based slurry propellant with no curing agent. It was about 80% solids (mostly AP), 17% HTPB binder and 3% DOA plasticizer, mixed by hand and then in a sigma mixer for about 45 minutes. Strands were cast, coated on the sides with silica grease so they burn from one face, and burned in a Crawford strand burner at 20–70 bar. A log–log regression of the burn rate against pressure gave the coefficient a and pressure exponent n in r = aPn.

Iron oxide raised the burn-rate coefficient from 1.42 to 1.75 mm/(s·barn). The exponent changed little, from 0.41 to 0.49, which is in the range expected for AP/HTPB propellants.

I also took part in Crawford burner tests of a lithium-perchlorate electrically controlled solid propellant (ECSP). Strands were held between a cathode and an anode, so they could be ignited and burned electrically, and the tests varied voltage, pressure and temperature. The report describes this setup and method but gives no numerical results.

Gel propellant droplet combustion

I formulated organic gels (aviation turbine fuel thickened with Thixotrol ST and xylene) and inorganic gels (fuel thickened with silica, with aluminum added in some mixes). Droplets were hung on a glass rod and burned under a high-speed camera, and their diameter history was analyzed with the D2-law.

  • Silica: raising it from 5% to 7.5% gave a stronger, more viscous gel. Lower silica gave a more fluid gel that reacted faster.
  • Aluminum, inorganic gels (10% silica): burning at 10% and 15% Al was unsteady (R2 of 0.72 and 0.75). At 25% Al the burning rate rose and the fit was best (R2 = 0.8998). At 30% Al the burn time stayed short, with R2 = 0.83.
  • Organic gels: without aluminum they burned steadily, with R2 above 0.98. More gellant and solvent raised the burning rate slightly, and adding 15% Al to the 7.5% gel raised it further.

Hybrid motor testing

I supported and observed the assembly and testing of an HTPB/N2O hybrid motor on a static test stand with a load cell, pressure transducers and a data acquisition system. Two injectors were compared, a showerhead and a swirl injector. A pyrotechnic igniter and a sequential timer controlled the ignition, valve opening and shutdown. I also reviewed equilibrium results for flame temperature and characteristic velocity across a range of oxidizer-to-fuel ratios. The report records the setup and method but gives no thrust results.

Sieving and X-ray inspection

I sieved ammonium perchlorate into coarse, medium and fine fractions with a tap-sieve shaker, since particle size affects burning rate and packing density. I also looked at an X-ray radiograph of a sorbitol/KNO3 (35/65) grain. It showed a mostly uniform structure with slight density variation in the middle and voids near both ends. Voids like these can cause hot spots and pressure spikes in a motor.

What these results are

These were exploratory laboratory studies with small numbers of tests, and the report gives trends and fitted constants rather than final performance claims. Some fits are moderate: the iron oxide slurry has an R2 of 0.91, and the aluminized gels range from 0.72 to 0.90. The work gave me hands-on practice in propellant preparation, instrumentation and data analysis.
