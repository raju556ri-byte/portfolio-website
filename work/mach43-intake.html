Mach 4.3 Single-Ramp Supersonic Intake

A shock-on-lip external compression intake designed with oblique shock theory in Python, then checked with 2D RANS simulations in ANSYS Fluent. It is the kind of inlet used in ramjets and scramjets.

Design method

The intake slows a Mach 4.3 freestream with an 11° compression ramp. The ramp shock lands exactly on the cowl lip (the shock-on-lip condition, which maximizes captured air), and a normal shock near the throat takes the flow to subsonic speed. I solved the θ–β–M relation numerically in Python to get the shock angle, then used the oblique and normal shock relations for the post-shock Mach number, static ratios and total pressure recovery. The capture height comes from continuity for 25 kg/s, and the throat height comes from isentropic area–Mach relations.

This gave a capture area of 1.26 m², a throat area of 0.57 m² and a ramp of 3.54 m horizontal length. The pressure ratio across the shock system is 2.90, and the total pressure loss is 11.26%.

CFD validation

I built a 2D model of the intake with a structured mesh, refined along the ramp, at the cowl lip and in the shock interaction zones, with inflation layers on the walls. The compressible RANS equations were solved in ANSYS Fluent with a density-based solver and the k–ω SST turbulence model, with air as a calorically perfect gas. Walls were no-slip and adiabatic. The solution was treated as converged when all residuals fell below 10-6 and the inlet and throat mass flows agreed within 0.5%.

The CFD mass flow is within 1.03% of the design value, so the capture area and ramp geometry ingest the intended airflow. Total pressure recovery is about 4.6% lower than the analytical value. The report attributes this to viscous dissipation and boundary layer growth, entropy generation across the lambda shock system, and local flow separation, none of which the inviscid theory includes.

Limits

The design is two-dimensional and for a single design point, with one ramp. Real-gas effects are ignored, which is reasonable at this Mach number. The report suggests multi-ramp inlets, three-dimensional effects and optimization for pressure recovery as next steps. Total pressure recovery was only compared at the design point, so off-design behaviour is not covered.
